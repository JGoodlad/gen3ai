# PROBE F — per-team gradient geometry of the R2 fold (2026-08-28)

**Question.** The owner proposes pairing **PCGrad**-style gradient surgery with **team-blocked
batching**. PCGrad only does anything where pairwise task-gradient cosines are **negative** — it
projects one task's gradient off another's, and where every pair is already positive the
projection is the identity. So the question is empirical: *do the nine R2 slice teams' loss
gradients conflict?*

**Answer in one line.** At the fold's **END** they do not — **0 of 36 pairs negative**, and PCGrad
would remove **exactly 0.000** of any team gradient. At the fold's **INIT** they do — **12 of 36
negative (11 robust across every resampling), min −0.248**, and PCGrad would remove up to **~32% of
a team gradient's norm**. The substrate is real but it is an **early-fold, trunk-localized**
phenomenon that ordinary averaging anneals away.

Registered predictions (ledger `f91de25`) are scored in §7 — **as registered**, and none adjusted.

## 3. Method — and why a raw cosine number would mean nothing

A batch gradient is (true team gradient + batch noise). Three constructions keep that honest:

1. **The noise ceiling.** Cosine between two battle-disjoint batches of the **same** team. This is
   the number every between-team cosine must be read against — it is what "perfectly aligned" looks
   like at this sample size.
2. **The noise-free estimator.** `⟨mean(folds 0,2,4), mean(folds 1,3,5)⟩` is unbiased for
   `⟨μ_i, μ_j⟩` **including i == j**, because the two halves' noises are independent. Everything
   derives from 108×108 (216×216) Gram matrices, one per parameter group, so no 1.4 GB matrix is
   ever held.
3. **Uncertainty by resampling.** All **10** balanced 3/3 fold splits give 10 independent estimates
   of the same cosine. A pair is called **conflicting** only if it is negative in *every* split.

**MISSING is never imputed.** A team whose own cross-half inner product is ≤ 0 has no resolvable
team-consistent gradient at this sample size; every cosine involving it is reported MISSING. A
parameter group a loss cannot reach *at all* (a policy CE has exactly zero gradient on the value
head) is labelled `structurally_zero` instead — a different fact with a different remedy.

**PCGrad arithmetic.** PCGrad projects `g_i` off each conflicting `g_j`, removing
`||g_i|·|cos_ij|` of **norm** and `cos_ij²` of **energy**. The per-team sum over conflicting `j` is
an **upper bound** — the sequential projections interfere — which is the right side to err on when
the verdict is "would this buy anything".

`0.135 · √0.035 ≈ 0.025` — **2.5 % of the full gradient's norm**, upper bound, on the group with
the *worst* conflict.

## 6. The PCGrad verdict arithmetic

| where | negative pairs | fraction of a team gradient's **norm** PCGrad would remove (upper bound) | fraction of **energy** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **END, whole vector, `distill`** | **0 / 36** | **0.000** | **0.000** |
| END, `policy_head` only | 4 / 36 | 0.135 (of that group) ⇒ ≤ **0.025 of the whole** | ≤ 0.00064 |
| END, `projection_pool` only | 4 / 36 | 0.100 (of that group) ⇒ ≤ 0.013 of the whole | ≤ 0.00018 |
| **INIT, whole vector, `distill`** | **12 / 36** | **0.324** (max team 0.882) | **0.052** |

Read literally: **at the fold's end there is nothing for PCGrad to do — the operation is the
identity.** At the fold's start it is a ~32 %-of-norm intervention on the fold's own primary loss,
concentrated in the trunk, on a balance-vs-offense split.

## 8. What this does and does not license

**Licenses:** an early-fold-only PCGrad arm is *not* operating on a null. If it is tried, it should
be (a) applied at the fold's first phase, where the conflict exists, (b) measured on the trunk,
where 82 % of the conflicting energy is, and (c) instrumented with this exact cross-half cosine so
the arm can be stopped once the conflict anneals — which the END measurement says it does, without
help.

**Does not license:** any claim that PCGrad would improve the fold. This probe measures **geometry,
not payoff**. Two distinct readings survive it and it cannot separate them: (i) the conflict is
real and resolving it faster is worth something, or (ii) the conflict is a transient that plain
averaging already resolves, and removing it early merely changes *which* shared solution is found.
Only a matched arm answers that.

**Also does not license** a "teams don't conflict, so raise team count freely" reading. The
domination-by-average finding (Prediction 2, ~50 % of energy in one direction at both epochs) is
the structural constraint here, and it is orthogonal to conflict — it is a statement about how
little of a team's gradient is team-*specific*, which is the same wall the COUNT-dominates-
conditioning and LUT-conditioning-ceiling results hit from the other side.

## 9. Caveats

1. **State-distribution confound (bounded, and conservative).** Each team's states come from *that
   team's own fork's* traces, so the batches differ in **policy** as well as in **team**. This can
   only *inflate* between-team gradient difference, so it makes the END "no conflict" verdict
   conservative and the INIT "conflict exists" verdict potentially overstated. Not repairable from
   the archive — the student's own traces cover the 719-team pool and carry too few decisions per
   slice team.
2. **The AWR weight is dropped** from the `distill` row (§2). The row *set* is exact; the
   *weighting* is flat.
3. **`eccfe630` has two teachers in the live fold** (F5a and F5e, KLs averaged); this probe uses
   F5a per the mission. It is the team with the strongest INIT conflict against `e0d97b0e`
   (F5e-taught), so this choice is not neutral for that one cell.
4. **`pg` is UNRESOLVED** (|cos| up to 27.8, ceiling +0.049) and `pgmc` is **partially** resolved
   (2 of 9 teams MISSING). Neither carries weight in any verdict. An exact PPO advantage is not
   recoverable offline; closing this needs the advantages logged during the fold, not more traces.
5. **Two points in parameter space, not a trajectory.** INIT and END bracket the fold; nothing here
   says where between them the conflict crosses zero.
6. **N = 9 teams.** The PC1-vs-1/9 comparison is over nine vectors; the isotropic null is exact but
   the estimate of PC1 from nine samples is not tight.
7. **`e0d97b0e` and `e541f7be` differ in one slot** (Jirachi vs Metagross) and are the two teams
   driving the INIT conflict block. A finding resting on a near-duplicate pair deserves the
   suspicion; the *other* five negative pairs in §7 do not involve both of them, so the block is
   not an artifact of that duplication.

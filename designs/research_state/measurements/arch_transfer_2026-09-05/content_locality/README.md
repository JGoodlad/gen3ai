# Content locality — is a specialist teacher's divergence LOCAL or GLOBAL?

**2026-09-05. Offline. No training, no launcher, no server.** Two eras, one statistic, ~29 CPU
minutes of battles in total.

The question the ledger needs answered: when a specialist teacher differs from the fold parent, is
that difference **concentrated on the teams the teacher was trained on** (LOCAL — team-specific
tactics, the leak-shaped content) or **spread evenly over teams it never saw** (GLOBAL — general
skill, the gift-shaped content)?

## Pre-registered prediction — and it is REFUTED, with the sign REVERSED

[`PREREGISTRATION.md`](PREREGISTRATION.md) was written and frozen before a single state was
generated. Its two claims:

* **H1 (cross-era)**: gen-era teachers (pointer-native head, physics in closed form) are **MORE
  local** than v8-era teachers (flat action head, refine loop) — `L_gen > L_v8`.
* **H2 (within-era)**: if locality predicts robbery, the **funded** teachers should be more local
  than the **unfunded** ones — `L_funded > L_unfunded`.

I also registered my own competing prediction (funded ≈ or slightly *less* local than unfunded,
because the funded fork's extra training is ordinary PPO that moves the whole policy).

**H1 is refuted and reversed: the v8-era teachers are the LOCAL ones and the gen-era teachers are
essentially GLOBAL.** H2 is NOT DETECTED. My own within-era prediction is not supported either —
the funded−unfunded difference is a null in both directions.

## The statistic

Forward **`KL(teacher ‖ parent)`** over legal actions, per decision state.

* Gen era: `masked_kl_rows` **imported** from `agents.training.instrumented_ppo.distill_anchor` —
  never reimplemented. It is the same formula the live `--distill-anchor-monitor` logs.
* v8 era: commit `b13b30b2` predates that module, so [`era_kl.py`](era_kl.py) carries a verbatim
  copy. [`kl_unit_test.py`](kl_unit_test.py) runs in the gen tree, where both are importable, and
  **gates the two bit-identical** on 7 synthetic cases (random logits, identical policies, an
  illegal action with a dominant logit, a single-legal-action row). Executed:

### Two readings, and why R is the primary one

| | what it is | what it controls for |
|---|---|---|
| **L** (the brief's form) | `mean KL on the teacher's own taught teams / mean KL on the untaught set` | nothing — a team set whose states simply make *any* two policies differ more inflates or deflates L |
| **R** (sibling control) | per taught team, `KL(own teacher) / mean KL(same-era sibling teachers)` on the **same states** | the team, the state distribution and the training recipe are all held fixed; only "did *this* teacher train on *this* team" varies |

**R is primary, and the floor is why.** The matched-noise floor below shows the team-set effect is
real and points in **opposite directions in the two eras** — so a cross-era comparison of L would
read the team sets, not the teachers. R cancels it exactly.

## The matched-noise floor (mandatory, and it changes the reading)

The identical statistic between two nearby same-run checkpoints of each era's **own parent**, on
the same states. This is the KL between two arbitrary near-identical policies.

| era | pair | KL on untaught | KL on taught | floor `L` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| gen | `ckpt_28067760` vs parent final (−47k steps) | 0.0374 | 0.0401 | **1.07** |
| gen | `ckpt_27917760` vs parent final (−197k) | 0.0654 | 0.0760 | **1.16** |
| v8 | `ckpt_277178472` vs parent final (−405k) | 0.0383 | 0.0263 | **0.69** |
| v8 | `ckpt_275758296` vs parent final (−1.82M) | 0.0664 | 0.0535 | **0.81** |

Two things follow.

1. **Nothing here is WITHIN FLOOR.** Every teacher's per-side aggregate KL is far above the
   floor on **both** sides: gen teachers span 0.489–1.023 against a 0.037–0.076 floor (≈6–27×),
   v8 teachers 0.219–0.472 against 0.026–0.067 (≈3–18×). Both sides of every ratio have
   something to measure, so no teacher is reported WITHIN FLOOR anywhere.
2. **The raw L is confounded and the floor proves it.** In the gen era two *arbitrary* checkpoints
   already differ ~1.07–1.16× more on the 16 taught teams than on the untaught 8; in the v8 era the
   effect runs the other way (0.69–0.81). So the gen teachers' raw `L ≈ 1.07–1.12` sits **at or
   below** the floor's own ratio — there is no locality left once the team set is accounted for —
   while the v8 teachers' `L ≈ 1.42–1.59` is *understated* by its floor. This is exactly why the
   sibling-control R is the headline.

## Verdict

**Cross-era (H1): SIGNIFICANT, and in the direction OPPOSITE the pre-registration.** v8-era
specialist teachers are genuinely LOCAL — their divergence from the fold parent is ~45% larger on
their own taught teams than their siblings' divergence on those same states (`R 1.4498
[1.2728, 1.6722]`), replicated at n=3 (`1.4601 [1.2467, 1.7347]`). Gen-era teachers are, on the
same instrument, **globally displaced**: unfunded `R 1.0723 [0.9803, 1.1634]` includes 1, funded
`R 1.1016 [1.0008, 1.1987]` clears it only marginally and does **not** clear it at n=3 (`1.1029
[0.9639, 1.2432]`), so gen-era locality itself is at best a small, non-robust effect. The era
difference is `+0.35…+0.38` with CIs excluding zero on both gen halves and at both n. **The
architecture story predicted gen-era teachers would be the local ones; the measurement says they
are the global ones, and it says so at a magnitude no reading of the CIs rescues.**

**Within-era (H2, the more decisive test because it has no architecture confound): NOT DETECTED.**
Funded minus unfunded `R = +0.0293 [−0.0267, +0.0808]` (n=9) and `+0.0111 [−0.0899, +0.1105]`
(n=3); on the raw L, `+0.0287 [−0.0665, +0.0941]` and `+0.0374 [−0.0936, +0.1484]`. **Locality does
not distinguish the teacher half that robs from the half that does not.** What *does* distinguish
them is plain magnitude: the funded teachers sit further from the parent on every team, taught and
untaught alike. If robbery is to be predicted from a teacher's divergence, this measurement points
at **how far** the teacher moved, not at **where**.

**Consequence for the "gift vs leak" frame.** The v8 fold — the one that GIFTED — was fed teachers
whose content was strongly team-local; the gen-era folds, which rob, are fed teachers whose content
is spread evenly across the pool. That is the reverse of the naive leak story ("local = idiosyncratic
= transfers badly"), and it is consistent with the mechanistic reading registered as the competing
one: a teacher can only displace the student off-slice if it *itself* differs off-slice, and the
gen-era teachers differ off-slice almost as much as on it. This measurement does **not** establish
causation — the two eras differ in architecture, exploiter recipe, teacher count, pool and parent
maturity all at once — but it does remove locality-as-cause from the gen side and hand the
"teacher content" line a concrete, measurable axis it did not have this morning.

## Findings and hazards hit

1. **The v8 teachers' taught sets are NOT disjoint.** `ai_v8_06_semistall_3team`'s
   `data/teams/sample/9d5f845869e899ee.txt` hashes to sha10 `564b9be3ae`, which is also
   `ai_v8_09_pool10`'s `t00`. The script's GIGO check caught it and would have hard-exited; it now
   dedupes the union and **excludes that one team from the sibling control** (21 singly-taught
   teams, not 22), because a team two of the three teachers saw has no clean "did not teach it"
   sibling. Anyone re-deriving the v8 taught union from the three argvs should expect 23 paths and
   22 teams.
2. **`untaught_probe.py` is referenced by a committed script but is not in the tree.**
   `reuse_batch_2026-09-03/offline_collateral_kl/offline_collateral_kl.py` reads the untaught-8
   list by regex out of a sibling `untaught_probe.py` that does not exist under `designs/`. The
   canonical run's own artifacts still name the teams (`untaught_C1_end.json`'s key order), which
   is how the list here was recovered — and the 1100-state cross-check confirms the recovery is
   exact, order included. But the script as committed **cannot run**, and the ordering that makes
   its seeds reproducible currently survives only inside a result file.
3. **`rev3_untaught_pulldown_selection.json` names a DIFFERENT untaught 8** from the one the reuse
   batch actually used (`a04c29cf…`, `e11829f0…`, `d4e74946…`, `b89e1e37…`, `8cdc78b2…` vs
   `61590463…`, `ce35b736…`, `f7ba5702…`, `90b94599…`, `dbf81d8e…`; only `9283210847f806ee` and
   `9d5f845869e899ee`/`9909f2e98e981ccc` overlap). Two sets are in circulation under the same name.
   This measurement uses the **reuse batch's** set, because that is the one every existing untaught
   number is measured on.
4. **The gen-era raw L is confounded and would have been mis-read.** Two arbitrary parent
   checkpoints already show floor `L` 1.07–1.16 on these team sets. A reader taking the gen
   teachers' `L ≈ 1.10` as "slightly local" would be reading the team sets. The sibling control is
   what makes the two eras comparable at all.
5. Not a defect, but worth recording: **the worktree needed `git submodule update --init` plus the
   two build-artifact symlinks** before any bridge battle ran, and the rust `sim_bridge` was taken
   from the main checkout via `$POKESIM_SIM_BRIDGE_BIN` rather than rebuilt (`src/rust_sim` last
   changed `e12741ad`, 2026-08-24 20:12; main's binary was built 2026-08-24 23:52, i.e. after it),
   so no `cargo build` saturated the box.

## Limits, stated plainly

* **State distribution is parent-piloted.** Every teacher is scored on states the *parent* reaches,
  not on states it would itself reach. That is the right choice for a divergence-from-parent
  statistic and it is what makes teachers comparable, but it is not the distribution the fold's
  own rollouts see, and these levels must never be merged with the live
  `distill/collateral_kl_vs_parent` column.
* **The two eras are different pools, teams, parents and recipes.** The v8 arm plays the 719-team
  era pool with a different fold parent, a different reference opponent, greedy play instead of
  seeded-stochastic, and the node bridge. The cross-era contrast is UNPAIRED and shares no team.
  R removes the team-set confound; it removes nothing else.
* **The cross-era comparison cannot attribute the difference to architecture.** Architecture,
  exploiter budget, teacher count (3 vs 8), teams per teacher (10/3/10 vs 2), pool and parent
  maturity all differ. "Gen-era teachers are less local than v8-era teachers" is measured; "because
  of the pointer head" is not.
* **Sibling count differs** (7 gen siblings vs 2 v8). A 2-sibling mean is noisier than a 7-sibling
  mean, which widens the v8 per-team R but does not bias it.
* **Two battles-per-team settings, not a pre-registered n.** n=3 was chosen to reproduce the
  canonical batch exactly; n=9 was run afterwards as a declared POWER EXTENSION once the n=3 CIs
  proved wide. Both are reported. No conclusion changes between them, and the one number that
  moves across the boundary (gen funded R clearing 1) is called out as non-robust rather than
  banked.
* **`L` for the gen halves is built on 2 taught teams per teacher.** Its per-teacher CI is
  meaningless at that cluster count; only the 8-teacher group bootstrap is quoted, and R (16 teams)
  is what the verdict rests on.

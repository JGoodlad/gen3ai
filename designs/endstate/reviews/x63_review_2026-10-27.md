## Verdict

- **The control is real and matched, as claimed.** X26c's immutable `original_command` is the design's argv
  without the mix. The fork parent is X26 @380,000,195 (sha `7e0ebe94…`), the pin history is one entry at `f4b25238`, and
  the 400M, 430M and 480M files the read needs are all on disk (§ What holds).
- **The experiment's decision layer is not ready to register.**
  - Its error rates rest on a σ that nobody has measured at +100M.
  - At plausible values of σ, the one-seed screen is close to a coin flip, yet its stop "retires" the lever for the
    era (B1).
- **Four claims in the design are wrong or unsupported:**
  - "X63 inherits X26c's learning-rate path": the rate is the KL controller's response to the lever itself (M1).
  - X63 "is the dose read" and can replace the strategy's fork extension. X63 changes four things at once, and its
    dose points are noise (M2).
  - "GUESS unchanged" when H-CTX is KILLED: the union then becomes a long N1-P read on the one panel N1-P barely
    covers (M3).
  - "Every engine option is fair to the contrast": not shown for an engine that changes our observation (M5).
- **The in-pool price has no working guard** (M4):
  - the S ceiling fires on the width of a Welch interval at about one degree of freedom (0.32 at a true cost of 0);
  - the home72 floor passes a drop of up to 7–8 pp.
- **A GAIN would be attributable to "a long continuation on this union at this share, under the production KL
  controller".** That bundle is a fair answer to the survey's question. It cannot say which component, the dose, the
  share or the learning rate carried the gain, and the design should stop claiming the dose part.

## BLOCKER

**B1. The decision rule's error rates rest on an unmeasured σ, and the one-seed screen "retires" breadth for the era on
what is close to a coin flip at plausible σ.**

*Why σ at +100M is not σ at 20M.* The rule's whole interval uses σ_eff = max(1.0 pp, σ̂), with σ̂ estimated from 2
degrees of freedom at Stage B. The only measured seed spreads come from 20M forks (C0 and D1, φ̂ 0.6–0.77, at
420–1,200 games a seed). PR-1 was designed to measure σ_s at 20M. A +100M continuation gives two seeds five times as
long to drift apart on a plateau the training meters call flat. Those meters see the pool; the drift that matters is
on F. MY JUDGEMENT puts σ anywhere in 0.5–3 pp.

*What happens across that range* (`power_review.log` A):

| σ (pp) | P(CONTINUE), true 0 / +2 pp | false GAIN | GAIN at +2 / +3 pp | RETIRE at a true +2 pp | INCONCLUSIVE, true 0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.19 / 0.82 | 0.001 | 0.46 / 0.91 | 0.18 | 0.00 |
| 1 | 0.29 / 0.72 | 0.026 | 0.40 / 0.70 | 0.28 | 0.03 |
| 2 | 0.38 / 0.63 | 0.072 | 0.26 / 0.38 | 0.37 | 0.17 |
| 3 | 0.41 / 0.59 | 0.070 | 0.15 / 0.20 | 0.41 | 0.28 |
| arm 2, control 1 (heteroscedastic) | 0.35 / 0.65 | 0.062 | 0.33 / 0.51 | 0.35 | 0.10 |

- **At σ ≥ 2 pp the screen barely separates a null from +2 pp** (continue 0.38 against 0.63). Its stop is then
  registered as RETIRE: "a 100M union continuation … is retired for goal (b) in this era". That is an era-level claim
  from one pair of seeds, wrong 0.37–0.41 of the time when the lever is worth +2 pp.
- **False GAIN sits at 0.06–0.08 for every σ ≥ 1.5 pp.** The design's FINDING 3 reports this, but only up to 2 pp.
- **The interval is honest only because of the 1 pp floor.** With a Student-t quantile on σ̂'s own degrees of freedom
  (4.30 at df 2), false GAIN falls to ≤ 0.022. Power at +2 pp then collapses to 0.07–0.15 at every σ. In other words,
  the registered power is bought by assuming σ ≤ 1 pp, not by measuring it.
- **The proposed σ triggers cannot fire reliably.**
  - X26c's "plateau wobble" (three checkpoints at 1,200 / 1,200 / 4,000 games) exceeds 1.5 pp 0.09 of the time with
    no wobble at all, and only 0.37 of the time when the true wobble IS 1.5 pp (E2).
  - PR-1's φ̂ measures 20M forks of P\*, not 100M continuations of X26@380M.

**Fix.**
1. **A Stage-A stop is NOT PURSUED, never RETIRE.** It records the achieved power and claims nothing for the era.
   RETIRE requires k ≥ 2 with UB < +1.5 pp.
2. **Register the interval's σ honestly.** Two acceptable forms:
   - a floor of 1.5 pp, with the power table re-run at that floor (the design already names 1.5 pp as its fallback);
   - or the t quantile on σ̂'s degrees of freedom whenever σ̂ exceeds the floor.

   State the false-GAIN rate at σ = 2 and 3 pp beside the headline.
3. **Measure σ where X63 can.**
   - The Stage-B control replicate (X63-C s6302, 19.9 GPU-h) against X26c is the only direct +100M control-control
     spread. Run it first at Stage B, not as the pair's second half.
   - If PR-1′ lands first, carry its σ_s as a lower bound, not an estimate.
4. **Drop the wobble trigger,** or play 4,000 games at each X26c checkpoint (≈ +8,400 games, ≈ 37–53 lane-h). At
   1,200 games a checkpoint it is noise.

## MAJOR

**M1. The learning rate is not inherited. It is the KL controller's response to the lever, and that is the variable
LR-F is testing.**

*What X26c ran* (read from its checkpoint sidecars):
- The KL controller was live (`lr_frozen` false; `target_kl` 0.01, `kl_factor` 2, `lr_factor` 1.2, bounds
  [1e-5, 6e-4]).
- The fork took X26@380M's rate, 8.37e-5. It held that rate from 381M to 471M and stepped one rung, to 6.98e-5, at
  472M.

*What the arm will do.*
- It starts from the same 8.37e-5.
- From its first rollout, 80 % of its teams are new. Every update is likely to move the policy more on unfamiliar
  states than on practised ones, and the controller divides the rate by 1.2 each time the realised KL exceeds 0.02.
- So the arm's rate path is an outcome of the lever, probably a lower one: the learning rate falls on exactly the
  content the lever adds.
- §9 says "X63 inherits X26c's learning-rate path, so its Δ is breadth at that path". That is false.
  `designs/training/step_size_and_batch.md` calls the controller's state "a hidden confound in every fold comparison"
  for this reason.
- LR-F, now on the card, tests whether this controller is what holds the plateau.

**Fix.**
- Correct §9: the lever is "breadth under the production KL controller", which is a legitimate bundle.
- Register `python -m main.dose` of both runs (sidecar source) as a REPORTED line beside Δ_F.
- Register in advance what a divergence means. For example: if the arm's median rate over 380–480M sits ≥ 2 rungs
  (1.44×) below X26c's, a GAIN or a stop carries the label "confounded with the controller's response", and the
  follow-up is a breadth arm at a frozen `--fork-lr` (its own registration).
- Write the PREREG after LR-F's look 1. If LR-F reads that the controller holds the plateau, the arm's controller path is
  the first thing to read.

**M2. X63 is not a dose read, yet it is offered as the replacement for the strategy's dose read and for the stack
fork. REVIEW_2 has already re-ranked on that basis.**

*Four things change at once against the 20M breadth forks:*
- the steps (100M against 20M);
- the parent (X26@380M against P\*@280M);
- the corpus share (0.8 against D3's 0.3 and D1's / N1-P's 0.5);
- the corpus (a union against one corpus).

*The two dose points cannot carry a dose claim.*
- The +20M and +50M points are played at 1,200 F games a model. A 1 v 1 Δ there has an SE of 2.04 pp (±4.0 pp at 95 %).
- The registered extension trigger ("Δ at +100M above Δ at +50M by ≥ 1 pp") fires on binomial noise alone with
  P = 0.33 (`power_review.log` E1).
- The strategy's fork extension (rank 6) holds the corpus, share and parent fixed and varies only the steps. That is
  what makes it a dose read. X63 has no same-corpus, same-share, same-parent point at a short dose.
- So a GAIN cannot say "dose" rather than "share 0.8" or "the union". A stop cannot say "not dose".

**Fix.** Choose one:
- **(a) Make X63 a dose read.** Play the +20M point (X63-U @400M and X26c @400M) at 4,000 F games each (+5,600 games,
  ≈ 24–35 lane-h). Register Δ(+100M) − Δ(+20M) within the pair as the dose contrast, with its SE (whether the seed
  term cancels between the two points of one lineage is UNVERIFIED, so it is REPORTED with both bounds). Move the
  extension trigger onto 4,000-game points.
- **(b) Drop the claims.** Remove "the dose explanation, made testable" (§1) and "recommended as its replacement" (§9,
  FINDING 1). Tell REVIEW_2's §6 that X63 answers "does a long union continuation move F", not "is dose the
  explanation".

**M3. The union's mix is set by convenience, and it dilutes the one component that F can read.**

*The registered shares.* Uniform over teams gives x62 0.357, n1 0.357, d3 0.086 and the pool 0.2. The design chose
this because `--team-mix` takes one corpus and "needs no code change", not because the question asks for it.

*Which components F can read.*
- F is the decider because it is clean of n1's generator. By the same argument it is where n1 shows least: n1 holds
  19 % of F's full sets, against 62 % / 56.5 % of H's / P72's.
- F is 70 % pool sets and 26 % recombined-like (X62 §1.2), so x62's context novelty is what F can see.
- So a third of the draws go to a component whose target panels (H, P72) the design has already declared
  undecidable.

*The U2 branch (H-CTX KILLED).*
- n1 rises to 0.516 and d3 to 0.124. X63 becomes a long N1-P on F at 0.65 of its corpus draws.
- §8 says "GUESS unchanged". Under the design's own reasoning the GUESS should fall towards the both-NULL row or
  below: the F-relevant component is gone, and the survey (§6.2, Packer) says procedural content helps only where it
  covers the test distribution.

*X62's corpus moved under X63.* X62's revised draft (`58cb02dc`, landed while this review was written) routes H-CTX
SUPPORTED and UNDECIDED alike, so X63's §8 and X62 now agree there. But its STRICT Hidden Power rule withdraws 86 of
the 658 pool sets and 6 species from the corpus. How much of F's pool-set share x62 still covers is UNVERIFIED, and
that coverage is the reason x62 is the F-relevant component.

**Fix.**
- Choose the mix by the question.
- Under U3, subsample n1 in the builder, which is a builder parameter and needs no code change. For example, 4,000
  n1 teams gives x62 0.64 / n1 0.21 / d3 0.15 of corpus draws (0.51 / 0.17 / 0.12 of all draws). That keeps set
  novelty in while F reads mostly context.
- Re-derive the GUESS and P(GAIN) for each union variant (U3, U2, no-n1, no-d3), not one GUESS "given the gates".
- Under U2, either do not run, or register U2 as "a long N1-P", with its own lower GUESS and its own P(GAIN) per
  resource-day.
- Re-measure F's coverage by the revised x62 corpus (the share of F's pool sets that survive the STRICT rule), and
  carry it into the GUESS.

**M4. The in-pool price has no working guard. The S ceiling fires on the width of its interval, and the home72 floor
passes a 7–8 pp drop.**

*The S ceiling* (`power_review.log` C). TRADE iff the Welch 97.5 % LB of Δ_S ≤ −6.0 pp, using D1's per-seed S SDs
(0.86 pp arm, 0.35 pp control).
- At k = 2, Welch's degrees of freedom are about one (median t quantile ≈ 8.7).
- P(TRADE) is therefore **0.32 at a true cost of 0**, 0.53 at the design's GUESS of −2.5 and 0.77 at −4.
- At k = 3 (t ≈ 3.5) it is 0.001 / 0.066 / 0.37.
- Stage B is where most GAINs land (k = 2). There, a third to a half of all GAINs go to the owner as TRADE whatever
  the true price: the rule penalises the uncertainty, not the cost.

*The goal-(a) floor* (`power_review.log` D). LB > 0.50 on the arm's pooled home72 passes at an observed 0.529 (1 seed,
1,200 games), 0.521 (2 seeds) or 0.517 (3 seeds). That is a drop of 6.9–8.1 pp from P\*'s 0.598. The design predicts
−2 (−5, +1), so the floor cannot bind inside the GUESS's 80 % range. It is the goal's definition, not a price guard.

**Fix.**
- Replace the ceiling with a price that is confidently paid: TRADE iff Δ_S's UB < −x (or iff the point estimate is
  ≤ −x). x is an exchange rate the owner writes BEFORE the read (the X62 review's B1 fix: "a GAIN on F of ≥ x pp is
  adopted at an in-pool cost up to y pp").
- X26c's home72 is re-played anyway (2,400 games at Stage A). Register Δ_home72 against X26c under the same
  exchange-rate rule, beside the floor, so that home72 against Kakuna is priced, not only floored.

**M5. "Every engine option is fair to the contrast" is not shown for options (a) and (c), and the preference order
puts the least faithful option first.**

*What the options mean.*
- Option (a) is PR-1's engine. Since REVIEW_2 that is "the tree pin", either the E1 hybrid or a frozen post-`b0441623`
  `main` commit. Option (c) is such a commit outright.
- A `main` commit plays `f4b25238`-trained finals across every play-path change since: `d8d674b99`'s silent volatile
  ends, the v163 Hidden Power belief, the consumed-item rule, the eval-mode load and the packer (REVIEW_2 §M1).
- Those changes alter OUR observation, so our greedy policy diverges from its training-engine play (`reads.md` §4:
  every protocol-changing divergence across E1 → E3 was our choice).

*Why the contrast is not automatically fair.*
- The divergence rate for the 10-26 / 10-27 changes on F is unmeasured. The ≈ 1 % in §10 item 9 is E1 vs E3 only.
- Whether a policy holds up under an input shift is the very property breadth training is meant to change.
- So the arm and X26c may lose different amounts to the shift. That is a treatment × engine interaction, of unknown
  size, against an effect of +1–3 pp.

**Fix.**
- Prefer (b), the E1 hybrid (REVIEW_2's option C, its PREFERRED), outright. Take PR-1's engine only if PR-1 chooses
  that hybrid.
- Under a fallback, register a per-model divergence measurement: for each final, a block of F units re-played on E1,
  choice by choice (G10).
- Register a HOLD if F's divergence rate exceeds an amount named in advance, or differs between the arm and the
  control by more than their binomial noise.

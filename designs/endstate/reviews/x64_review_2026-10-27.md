## Verdict: **DROP** (as designed). Re-entry is a REDESIGN on a named condition (§ Recommendation)

- **The parts that hold** (§ What holds):
  - the choice of recombined over procedural teams to isolate the count;
  - the equal-draw shares;
  - the nested arms;
  - the rejection of one seed per arm;
  - the SHRINKS power arithmetic, which reproduces.
- **The read cannot answer its own question.**
  - The primary G rewards the in-pool loss the design predicts. Its only licensing class, SHRINKS-U, fires about as
    often when breadth buys no U at all as when the design's own GUESS is true (B1).
  - With a primary that can tell the two apart, the toy has no power at the effect it can produce (B2).
- **It costs more than the production reads it would screen.**
  - X63's Stage A costs 19.9 GPU-h and X53-N1's 39.4. X64 costs ≈ 50.
  - Neither of those waits on X64 (B3).
- **The design's own claims need correcting:**
  - matched steps pre-plateau push toward SHRINKS-HOME, not toward FLAT (M1);
  - the revised X62 corpus no longer trains "the same 658 sets" (M2);
  - home72 is the trainee's 10 % bias set (M3);
  - the K0 gate is weak, and it gates the wrong quantity (M4);
  - transfer is weaker than §6.1 says (M5);
  - the opponent side's team type moves with N, and the design does not name it (M6).

## BLOCKER

**B1. The primary rewards the in-pool price, and SHRINKS-U, the only licensing class, is a coin flip on U.**

*The arithmetic of G.* G = home72 − U. In X62's revised design the in-pool cost is a registered PRICE:
net = Δ_home72 + r · Δ_U, with r = 1 the default (`58cb02dc` §5.2). In that notation, X64's Δ is
**Δ_home72 − Δ_U, the same quantity with r = −1**. A U gain and a home72 loss both count as success. X64's own
predictions (§8) put five sixths of the predicted Δ on the home side: Δ_home −2.5, Δ_U +0.5. The design knows this
(§10 item 1) and routes SHRINKS on the point sign of Δ̂_U. But Δ_U's 90 % half-width is 4.2 pp at 3 / 2 / 3
(`review_power.log` (2)), which is 8× the predicted Δ_U.

*What the registered rule does* (`review_power.log` (2); 3 / 2 / 3, σ_g 2 pp, level σ 3 / 2 pp):

| truth over the range | P(SHRINKS) | **P(SHRINKS-U)** | P(SHRINKS-U \| SHRINKS) | P(U-GAIN) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| null | 0.04 | 0.025 | 0.58–0.64 | 0.04 / 0.01 |
| **pure starvation: home −3, U 0** | 0.33–0.35 | **0.18–0.20** | 0.55–0.57 | 0.04 / 0.01 |
| starvation + a slower broad arm: home −4, U −1 | 0.35 | 0.12–0.14 | 0.35–0.40 | 0.02 / 0.00 |
| **the design's §8 GUESS: home −2.5, U +0.5** | 0.34–0.35 | **0.22–0.24** | 0.63–0.68 | 0.06 / 0.02 |
| the design's §5 model: home −1.5, U +1.5 | 0.35 | 0.26–0.29 | 0.76–0.84 | 0.12 / 0.07 |
| **breadth buys U at no price: home 0, U +3** | **0.34–0.35** | 0.31–0.34 | 0.91–0.97 | 0.27 / 0.24 |
| breadth buys U, with a price: home −3, U +3 | 0.82–0.83 | 0.73–0.79 | 0.89–0.96 | 0.27 / 0.25 |

- **SHRINKS-U carries almost no evidence that U moved, at the sizes the design predicts.** Under pure starvation
  it reads 0.18–0.20, about the design's own prior for SHRINKS-U (0.20, §8). Under the GUESS it reads 0.22–0.24, a
  likelihood ratio of ≈ 1.2.
- **The routing is biased toward -U.** P(SHRINKS-U | SHRINKS) is 0.55–0.64 even when U's true change is exactly
  0, because U's noise enters Δ̂ with a minus sign. A SHRINKS that happens by luck tends to come with a lucky Δ̂_U > 0.
- **G cannot tell the two worlds apart.** "U +3 at no in-pool price", the best outcome breadth could have, and
  "home −3 with U unmoved", the worst, read SHRINKS at the same rate.
- **§6.2 acts on SHRINKS-U.** It licenses production breadth GPU (X62, N1-P, X53-N1, rec 2) and turns a production
  NULL into "dose". So a starved pool would buy production GPU about one time in five.
- **G_ctx = home72 − rc72b is not the cure.** It has home72 on the same side, so the starvation term enters it
  identically. It also gains from team-type familiarity, because rc72b's sixes are the corpus generator's held-out
  draws and A-12.7k trains on 94 % such sixes (M6). It has more confounds than G, not fewer.

**Should U alone be primary?** Yes, or the U-reachable panel F, with home72 as a registered price. U is goal (b)'s
quantity, and starvation cannot raise it. The price comes from X62's revised form: net = Δ_home72 + r · Δ_U, with the
owner's r. G stays as a REPORTED line, because it is the literature's statistic.

**Fix.**
- The primary is Δ_U (one-sided, LB > 0, with the 3 pp level floor), or Δ_F with Δ_U reported.
- home72 enters as a price.
- SHRINKS-U and SHRINKS-HOME are deleted as routing classes.
- B2 is what that change costs.

**B2. With a primary that can tell the worlds apart, the toy cannot answer at the effect it can produce.**

U-GAIN power (`review_power.log` (3); home −3 throughout, level σ 3 pp, σ_g 2 pp):

| seeds (runs, GPU-h) | half-width of Δ_U | U +1 | U +2 | U +3 | U +5 | false (U 0) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3 / 2 / 3 (8, ≈ 50) | 4.2 pp | 0.08 | 0.16 | 0.27 | 0.55 | 0.04 |
| 4 / 0 / 4 (8, ≈ 50) | 3.7 pp | 0.09 | 0.19 | 0.33 | 0.66 | 0.04 |
| 6 / 0 / 6 (12, ≈ 76) | 3.0 pp | 0.12 | 0.25 | 0.45 | 0.82 | 0.04 |

**What effect can the toy produce?** Three things bound it:
- **The toy's own gap is barely there.** At T\* (290M) home72 − F is 3.5 pp, and with n = 400 games on each side
  that is [−3.4, +10.4], not detected (X51: 0.493 against 0.458). At 100M the design guesses G ≈ +4.
- **Recombination reaches about one third of U.** H and P72 carry 62–69 % sets outside the pool (X62 §1.2). Even a
  complete closure of F's context share moves U by about a third of F's movement, plus whatever team-level skill
  lifts H and P72.
- **The design's own GUESS is Δ_U +0.5 [−2.5, +3.5].** A realistic true Δ_U is 0 to +2 pp, where even 12 runs read
  U-GAIN 0.12–0.25.

**Fix.** None at a screen's cost. Four to six seeds an endpoint is X53-N1's GPU or more, and that buys the
production question directly (B3). That is why the recommendation is DROP and not REDESIGN-now.

**B3. Decision value: X64 is dominated by the reads it would gate.**

| read | GPU-h to its first decision | lane | answers | waits on X64? |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| **X64, 3 / 2 / 3** | ≈ 50 (+ 1.5 agent-days) | ≈ 103–147 lane-h (≈ 39,000 toy games; REVIEW_2 §3) + K0 | a toy curve; transfer LOW (survey) | — |
| X63 Stage A | **19.9** | 66–95 | the production union continuation at +100M, against a free, matched control | **no** (X63 §8, FINDING 12) |
| X53-N1 Stage A (L100) | **39.4** | 69–99 | production from step 0: the pool against 10⁴ teams at 100M. These are X64's two endpoints, at production | no |
| the survey's own rec 5 | ≈ 19 | ≈ 27 | the same curve, one seed per arm | — |

- **X64 costs more than the reads it gates.** §6.2's licence for SHRINKS-U is that X63 or X53-N1 "is licensed to ask
  for its GPU". X63's Stage A costs 40 % of X64's GPU and asks the production question with a matched control. X63
  has already said it will not wait on X64.
- **The from-step-0 question, X64's unique angle, has a production read.** X53-N1 Stage A answers it at 79 % of
  X64's GPU, with no transfer step. The N4 review's M6 made the same equal-cost argument for N4-P against the N4 toy.
- **Of the design's claimed uniques, only the corpus-size answer (φ̂) remains.** It cannot be read at two A-3.7k
  seeds (m3), and it does not transfer across capacity (M5).
- **The lane is saturated.** REVIEW_2 measured the whole box at 390–545 Kakuna games an hour. Ahead of PR-1′ the queue
  already holds ≈ 84,000–122,000 games, and X64's ≈ 39,000 toy games come on top. At the toy's ≈ 1.66× per-game speed
  that is ≈ 43–60 h of the WHOLE box, behind N1-P, D3, X48, X62 and X63.
- **The survey priced rec 5 at ≈ 19 GPU-h + ≈ 27 lane-h**, with transfer confidence LOW. X64 correctly found that
  price unreadable (its FINDING 1). The readable form costs 2.6× the GPU and 4–5× the lane, and B2 shows it is still
  unreadable on U.

**Fix.** DROP. The re-entry condition is in § Recommendation.

## MAJOR

**M1. Matched steps on a rising toy: team count IS pilots per team, and the bias runs toward SHRINKS-HOME.**
- **Collinear by construction.** At 100M every arm plays ≈ 2.85M games. Pilots per team per side are 2.85M / N:
  ≈ 3,950 (A-719), ≈ 760 and ≈ 220. N and per-team exposure are one axis, and no matched-steps design can separate
  them.
- **The literature's identification is missing here.** CoinRun had the same collinearity. There it was harmless,
  because the IMPALA train score stayed at 97.7–99.4 % across level counts, so the gap closed on the test side. X64
  predicts its train side falls (§8: Δ_home −2.5), so the gap's movement is no longer identified as a test-side gain.
  That is B1.
- **Pre-plateau makes it worse, in the direction §10.3 denies.**
  - At 100M, A-719 sits near X52's practice dose (≈ 4,000 pilots) on home72, and A-12.7k sits at ≈ 220 (≈ 425 for
    home72 teams, M3).
  - The broad arm also learns more slowly early (Procgen's implicit curriculum; the X61 review's m5).
  - Both push the home side DOWN in A-12.7k and keep U from rising. The first produces SHRINKS-HOME. The second
    hides SHRINKS-U.
  - §10.3 says "A SHRINKS at 100M is, if anything, conservative". That holds only for a U-driven SHRINKS, and B1
    shows the rule cannot tell which kind it got.
- **The toy is still rising.** Toy C0 at T\* + 70M beats T\* head to head by +2.0 pp (the N4 review's M5), so even
  290M is a soft plateau. At 100M every arm is on the slope.
- **The dose flag is blind.** DOSE-LIMITED (§4.5) compares in-house S_U from 75M to 100M. X37b showed that in-house
  meters cannot see the familiarity gap (P\*'s in-house shortfall +4.0 [−0.7, +8.4] against Kakuna's ≈ 11 pp), and
  S_U's reference population R trained on the pool. "Still climbing on S_U" is not "still climbing on U".

**Fix.**
- Correct §3.1's bias paragraph and §10.3.
- Print the pilots per team for home72 in each arm beside every class.
- If the read is ever run, the dose flag reads Kakuna F at 50M and 75M for the two endpoints (≈ 1,200 games a model).

**M2. The revised corpus is not "the same 658 sets in every arm".** X62's revised draft (`58cb02dc` §2.2) adopts the
STRICT Hidden Power rule.
- **What the corpus draws.** 572 of the pool's 658 sets and 63 of its 69 species. Arcanine, Blaziken, Dragonite,
  Glalie, Linoone and Weezing occur only in the no-IV-line form. The corpus's set-exposure total variation from the
  pool is 0.173.
- **The withdrawn sets live only in pool teams.** So their share of draws falls with the pool share: 100 % in A-719,
  19 % in A-3.7k and 5.65 % in A-12.7k. That is a ≈ 94 % cut in exposure to 86 sets and 6 species, perfectly
  correlated with N.
- **This is the set-level confound §2.1 chose recombination to avoid.** It would show on any panel that holds those
  sets or species, on our side or Kakuna's.
- **home72 holds none of the 86** (X62 rev. §2.2), so the cut lands only on the U side. It biases Δ_U down, toward
  SHRINKS-HOME or GROWS.
- §0 ("all three arms train on the same 658 sets") and §2.1 ("the pool's 658 sets in every arm") are false under the
  corpus X64 inherits.

**Fix.**
- Correct §0 and §2.1.
- Report each panel's share of slots carrying one of the 86 sets or 6 species.
- Report Δ_U restricted to teams without them.
- Or wait for X62's "complete the IV line" alternative (its FINDING 9), which keeps all 658 sets.

**M3. home72 is the trainee-side sample-bias set, so G's trained side is double-dosed (verified).**
- **The evidence.**
  - `heldout_panels.json` registers home72 as "all 72 sample teams (no draw)", sourced from `data/teams/sample`,
    which holds exactly 72 team files.
  - The toy's trainee draw is `default_biased` with `bias_prob` 0.1 over the sample teams
    (`rb_toy_ent_s4002/metadata.json`, `_matchup_spec`).
  - The opponent side is the uniform pool.
- **Trainee-side draw rate per team** (arithmetic from the shares):

  | arm | a home72 team | another pool team | a corpus team | home72 vs a typical team |
  |---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
  | A-719 | 0.9 / 719 + 0.1 / 72 = 2.64e-3 | 1.25e-3 | — | **2.1×** |
  | A-12.7k | 0.0565 × 2.64e-3 = 1.49e-4 | 7.1e-5 | 7.9e-5 | **1.9×** |

- **What it does.**
  - The ratio is nearly constant across arms, so it does not bias the slope directly.
  - But G's "trained context" is the 72 most-practised teams, not a typical trained context. "N equally weighted
    contexts" (§2.2) fails for exactly the panel the read uses.
  - It inflates the level G and K0 measure.
  - It makes home72's fall in A-12.7k a fall from double the typical practice.
- This resolves the design's FINDING 9 ("their count was not checked") and the X62 review's m7 (UNVERIFIED there).

**Fix.**
- Add a non-sample in-pool panel: 72 teams drawn, seeded, from the other 647, registered like home72 as an in-pool
  reference.
- Report G on both.
- Say in G's definition that home72 is the bias set.

**M4. The K0 gate passes too rarely where it matters and gates the wrong quantity.** K0 PASS iff Ĝ ≥ +2.0 and
Ĝ_ctx ≥ +1.0, as point bars at SEs of 1.4 and 1.7 pp (`review_power.log` (4)):

| true (G, G_ctx) | (0, 0) | (1, 0.5) | (2, 1) | (3, 1.5) | **(4, 2), the design's point** | (6, 3) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| P(K0 PASS) | 0.02 | 0.09 | 0.25 | 0.47 | **0.67** | 0.88 |

- **It HOLDs a third of the time at the design's own prediction**, after spending 19–27 lane-h.
- **It sizes G, not what X64 needs.** After B1, what matters is how much U-side headroom recombination could close,
  roughly the context part of home72 − F. G's level includes H and P72's set novelty, which recombination cannot
  reach.
- **It is double-dosed (M3), and it measures another toy.** It plays the old seeds at `3899277a`, before the bf16
  trunk (X61 FINDING 8).

**Fix.** If an X64-like read is ever registered:
- K0 bars on 90 % LBs of home72 − F and of the non-sample panel's premium (M3);
- reported as a planning ceiling, the way the X61 review's B2 asked.

**M5. Toy-to-production transfer is weaker than §6.1 claims.**
- **The threshold depends on capacity.**
  - CoinRun: "Bigger networks generalise better at every size".
  - Procgen: IMPALA at ×2 and ×4 width improves both train and test (survey §2).
  - So WHERE a curve closes is a property of the network. The toy is the 1.42M-parameter, `D_MODEL` 128, 2-round
    network with no belief stack (X53 §4; the N4 review's M6).
  - φ̂'s "≈ 3k teams suffice" and "a larger corpus is needed" are toy-capacity statements, and §6.2's corpus-size
    column does not transfer.
- **A toy FLAT confounds capacity with breadth.** A small network may be unable to use 12,700 contexts. The N4 review
  called the toy "the place LEAST likely to be capacity-bound" for in-house strength. The open question here is
  different: whether it can hold 12,700 contexts at all. X48 and N4 are reading capacity now. Until they read, a toy
  FLAT has two explanations and no way to choose.
- **The toy plays near or below 0.5 on its own teams.** T\* reads 0.493 on home72 and 0.458 on F (X51). At 100M it is
  weaker still. Premiums measured near a floor compress, so the toy's G is a lower-resolution version of P\*'s, not
  half of it.
- **"The comparison is internal" helps the sign, not the size or the threshold.** The design's §6.1 judgement is fair
  for the sign of a U-driven SHRINKS, and that is the class B1 shows it cannot identify.
- **Toy calibration is NOT SHOWN** (X35). The survey rates transfer LOW.

**Fix.** A toy read licenses nothing negative about production, and no corpus size. A redesign may license only a
positive, U-side SCREEN-GO, as the X61 review's B2 required.

**M6. The opponent's teams move with N too, so the team-type confound is two-sided.**
- `--team-mix-sides both` makes 94 % of A-12.7k's OPPONENT teams recombined sixes as well as the trainee's. The
  trainee mostly faces less coherent teams (the ≤ 2 rule breaks the pool's 309 frequent cores, §2.1).
- Kakuna pilots human-built teams. So the arms differ in what they learn to face, not only in what they pilot.
- §10 item 2 names only the trainee side.
- rc72b is the generator's own held-out draw, so A-12.7k plays it as an in-distribution team type. Δ_ctx therefore
  carries "learned recombined-team play" by construction.
- The design's diagnostic, rc72b against F, cannot separate the two sides.

**Fix.**
- Name the two-sided confound in §8 and §10.
- G_ctx is never a decider or a co-primary (this answers the design's FINDING 10: do not promote it).
- A trainee-side-only arm is the attribution read, and it is outside a screen's budget.

## MINOR

- **m1. The power model contradicts the predictions.**
  - §5 simulates half of every change on each side and A-719 G = +5. §8 predicts five sixths on home72 and G ≈ +4.
  - The SHRINKS column reproduces exactly under §5's model (`review_power.log` (1)).
  - P(FLAT) under a null reproduces at **0.13 (G +5) and 0.09 (G +4)**, not the doc's 0.16–0.20. The design's script
    is not committed (its FINDING 5), so the difference cannot be traced.
  - **Fix:** the PREREG simulates §8's own split and level, with a level σ.
- **m2. FLAT fires only on a positive point estimate.** LB > −2.0 at a half-width of 3.6 pp needs Δ̂ ≥ +1.6 pp: the gap's
  point estimate must GROW. It is calibrated (≈ 0.05 at a true −2), but "breadth from step 0 loses its toy
  evidence" (§6.2) would rest on a draw where the gap's point estimate rose by 1.6 pp. **Fix:** say so in the class
  text.
- **m3. φ̂ cannot be read.**
  - Each arm mean of G has SE ≈ 1.5 pp (3 seeds) to 1.9 pp (2 seeds), at 2.67 pp per final.
  - So φ̂ = (Ḡ_3.7k − Ḡ_719) / (Ḡ_12.7k − Ḡ_719) has a numerator of ≈ −1.7 ± 2.4 over a denominator of ≈ −3 ± 2.2.
    Its "≥ 0.8" and "≤ 0.3" routes are noise.

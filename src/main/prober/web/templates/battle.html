how to read a turn card
V
The critic's expected shaped, discounted return from this state. Its zero is not "even" — a measured self-mirror 50/50 reads about −6.5 — so read it against that offset, not against 0.
ΔV
How far V moved to the next decision. A large negative step is a value cliff: the moment the critic's read of the game fell apart. It is what scan ranks battles by.
P(win)
The win-prob head's calibrated probability of winning — the readable complement to V. Here 50% really is even. Absent on runs without that head.
ΔP
How much the move moved the odds, in percentage points (a difference of two probabilities is not a "%"). +1.3pp = 82% → 83.3%.
TD δ
The critic's surprise: δ = r + γV(s′) − V(s). Near zero = the turn went as expected; large negative = much worse than it predicted.
r
The environment reward for the step, across every shaping term. ⓘ means the per-term breakdown is in details.
P(win) · dist
A different estimator of the same question: the distributional critic's own mass above zero, drawn as a bar with the 50% mark on it so the crossing is visible. The bar turns red and gains a rail once the model is calling the game — from the first decision whose P(win) stays under 50% to the end. tail beside it is the catastrophic-band mass: piling up while V still looks healthy is the stall signature, and it is exactly what a single number cannot show.
expect
What the model thought the opponent was about to do (the α/β heads) — a belief, not the board. The one line separating a turn played AROUND a threat from one it never saw coming.
analyze
Opens the full forensic read of that decision — faithfulness, beliefs, threat tables, intervention, saliency. It loads the checkpoint, so it works on a run at the current architecture and diagnoses the drift on any other.
